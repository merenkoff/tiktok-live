## ShopKeep (iPad POS, 2008–2020s): what small shops loved, and how it was "sunset"

### Takeaway
ShopKeep was born from a Brooklyn wine-shop owner's pain. His server-based POS crashed while he was on vacation, and he could not see reports unless he stood in the store. It won small merchants with four things:
- an iPad of about $500 instead of a Windows terminal of about $3,000
- cloud data, so the owner saw today's sales and the cash in the drawer from his phone (the Pocket app, 2015)
- easy setup and training
- 24/7 support

Lightspeed bought it in November 2020 for about $440M. Lightspeed's own support page still says the product "is not changing and will continue to be supported and maintained", as Lightspeed Retail (S-Series). Third-party reviews disagree: they call it retired, with users "basically forced to migrate". I found no hard sunset date.

### Cited Findings
- **Founding.** ShopKeep was co-founded in fall 2008 out of a Brooklyn wine store by Jason Richelson and Amy Bennett. The first iPad version "was released in the App Store" in August 2011 (this sentence is uncited on Wikipedia). By July 2015 more than 23,000 small businesses used it, mostly retail, coffee shops, restaurants and bars. [V] — [Wikipedia: ShopKeep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShopKeep)
- **The pain behind it.** Richelson ran Green Grape in Fort Greene. His data sat on a server, so he had to be in the store to check reports, and terminal makers gave him little support. In his words: "The final nail in the coffin was when I was on vacation and the server crashed in the store". He called the iPad "a catalyst for making innovative point of sale systems much more affordable for merchants". The article puts a Windows touch-screen POS at about $3,000 against about $500 for an iPad. Price in 2014: $50/month per register, with processing paid separately. Over 10,000 customers. He also said: "I truly believe in five years you're not going to see any more PCs behind the counter". [V] — [American Banker, 19 Mar 2014](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/how-a-mobile-payment-idea-fermented-in-a-wine-shop)
- **2014 state.** $49/month per register; "over 10,000 stores"; "over $1.8 billion in receipts". ShopKeep sold a stand, a swiper and a printer, and set customers up remotely. Richelson quotes:
  - "Now in the palm of your hand you can see how much you've rung up in sales, and how much is in your cash drawer."
  - "Storeowners understand in real time how their business is running."
  - "There are millions and millions of transactions that run on Windows XP and are going to have to get replaced,"

  [V] — [TechCrunch, 24 Apr 2014](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/24/shopkeeps-point-of-sale-software-rings-up-25-million)
- **Pocket app (2015).** A free iOS companion app with daily sales totals, customer counts, hourly sales, top sellers, a comparison against prior days, and several locations in one view. Android followed in 2016, so owners could see performance "without logging into the full back-office system". [S] — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/shopkeep-debuts-iphone-app-to-track-sales-data-from-ipad-pos-system/), [FSR](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/industry-news/shopkeep-pocket-mobile-app-debuts-ios/), [PaymentsJournal](https://www.paymentsjournal.com/pocket-app-from-shopkeep-now-available-on-android/)
- **Support and offline (reviews).** Free 24/7 phone and email support, plus chat during business hours [S] — [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/compare-products/vend-vs-shopkeep-pos). According to a search summary of TechRadar's review, the app kept working during an internet outage and uploaded stored data once the connection returned. The same summary says ShopKeep is no longer available to new customers after the Lightspeed acquisition. [S] — [TechRadar review](https://www.techradar.com/reviews/shopkeep-pos-review) (the page body did not load for me, so these are not verbatim quotes). A liquor-store reviewer said it is not industry-specific and lacks a few features for that trade [S] — [SoftwareSuggest](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/shopkeep).
- **Acquisition.** Announced 5 Nov 2020: about $440M total ($145.2M cash plus 9.5M shares). ShopKeep had about $50M revenue and about $7B in gross transaction volume for the 12 months to Sept 2020. Lightspeed CEO Dax Dasilva promised "even more crucial product innovation and world-class support". The release does not say whether the product will be kept or retired. [V] — [Lightspeed news](https://lightspeedhq.com/news/lightspeed-acquires-shopkeep). The deal closed 25 Nov 2020. [V] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShopKeep)
- **Official status: still maintained.** Lightspeed's support page says:
  - "Our point of sale is not changing and will continue to be supported and maintained."
  - "You do not need to switch products."
  - "ShopKeep isn't going anywhere."
  - "you won't notice any changes in your day-to-day usage."

  The product is branded Lightspeed Retail (S-Series), and the shopkeep.com site and blog redirect to LightspeedHQ.com. [V] — [ShopKeep support: "ShopKeep is Now Lightspeed"](https://shopkeep-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/47479980352539-ShopKeep-is-Now-Lightspeed). An App Store listing "Lightspeed Retail POS (S)" still exists [S] — [App Store id434906089](https://apps.apple.com/app/id434906089).
  - **Contradicted by a 2026 review.** It lists the status as "Retired (now Lightspeed Retail)" and says "existing users were basically forced to migrate over to" Lightspeed Retail. It gives no date and quotes a user: "Our team transitioned from Shopkeep without too many issues." It also says the price rose from about $49 to about $69/month. [V] — [ecommerce-platforms.com](https://ecommerce-platforms.com/articles/shopkeep-review)
  - **Also contradicted** by a software directory that calls it "maintenance mode", with users "gradually being encouraged to migrate" [S] — [Softabase](https://softabase.com/en/software/pos/shopkeep).
- **Lightspeed's consolidation context.** On the fiscal Q4 2023 call (18 May 2023), 70% of new customer locations were on the two flagship platforms (Lightspeed Retail and Lightspeed Restaurant), and the sales force was selling almost only those two [S] — [MarketBeat transcript](https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2023-5-18-lightspeed-pos-inc-stock). Lightspeed later sold the non-core Upserve U.S. line, which it had bought for $430M, to Skyview Equity for up to $81M, covering about 3,200 locations [S] — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/lightspeed-offloads-upserve-at-a-discount-as-transformation-plan-continues/), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/lightspeed-commerce-sells-upserve-u-150207246.html), [tech.co](https://tech.co/news/lightspeed-acquires-upserve).
- **A migration path out.** One merchant wrote that they were "originally Shopkeep POS users, which then became Lightspeed S-Series", and that they were moving to Shopify [S] — [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/newly-migrated-to-shopify-from-lightspeed/234931/6).

### Inferences
- **The ShopKeep moment to recreate is not a screen. It is the owner standing outside the store and still knowing two numbers:** today's takings and the cash in the drawer. That was Richelson's founding pain and the pitch of Pocket. For a Ukrainian grocer the 2026 equivalent is a phone view or Telegram digest: totals, cash expected in the drawer, hourly curve, top items, "vs. same weekday last week", and a push when the Z-report closes.
- **"Cheap generic hardware plus a monthly fee per register"** ($49–69) is the business-model moment. Small grocers will compare that against a one-off $100–300 electronic cash register (ECR) (see the ECR section).
- **The sunset pattern** is consolidation rather than a dated shutdown: freeze the acquired product, sell only the flagships, divest or migrate the rest. Merchants experience this as uncertainty, not as one announcement. This is an inference from the conflicting sources above.

### Gaps
- No verbatim quotes from deli, market or grocery ShopKeep merchants. Reddit is blocked, the Lightspeed testimonials page (`/reviews`) returned 404, and the HN search for "ShopKeep" returned only "shopkeeper" hits.
- No official ShopKeep/S-Series end-of-life date was found. If one exists, it would be in Lightspeed account notices that are not public.
- I verified no details of the register screen itself. [R] I recall department/category item buttons, quick-tender cash buttons and an "open item" button; treat that as unverified.

## Square Register era (2009–2014): reader, receipts, signature, item grid, Square Stand, Square Market — which choices became standard and which were lost

### Takeaway
Square's lasting moments are these:
- The tiny headphone-jack reader (2010).
- The emailed receipt, already in beta in Dec 2009, carrying the merchant's logo, email, Twitter handle, a Google Map of where you paid, and your finger signature.
- A free iPad register (announced May 2011, rebuilt in March 2012) whose item layout "mirror[s]" the store, with one-tap print and cash-drawer actions, employee permissions and "Google-Analytics-style" reports.
- Square Stand (May 2013, $299), which swivels between merchant and customer.

Emailed/SMS receipts, tablet-on-a-stand registers, the customer-facing flip (for signature, and with it the tip prompt), and free software with near-cost hardware all became standard. The map on the receipt, the consumer directory and menus, and finger signature itself were lost. Signature lost to EMV/PIN, which is [R].

The emailed receipt was a link to a Square page "featuring the logo of the vendor, their email address, and their Twitter handle", plus amount and time, and "below that is a Google Map of where the transaction was made and your signature". SMS receipts were planned. His verdict: "this absolutely should be the way of the future". On signing: "It’s also humorous how bad my signature is when done with my finger on an iPod touch screen." [V] — [TechCrunch, 1 Dec 2009](https://techcrunch.com/?p=124666)

The 2013 successor was thinner and more robust [S] (Fast Company). One profile says the Reader was "Inducted into the pantheon of great designs at the Museum of Modern Art in New York", with no date or source given [V] — [Develop3D](https://develop3d.com/profiles/hip-to-be-square-hardware-tech-product-design/).

### Inferences
- **Became industry standard:**
  - digital receipts by email/SMS
  - the merchant's branding on the receipt
  - a tablet on a swivelling stand as "the register"
  - the customer-facing turn of the screen, which carried signature and then the tip prompt
  - free POS software subsidised by payments
  - real-time analytics by hour and weekday
  - an item layout the owner arranges to mirror the shop
- **Lost:**
  - the Google Map on the receipt ([R] I do not believe current Square receipts show one; unverified)
  - the receipt as a page with the merchant's social handles
  - the consumer app's merchant directory and menus
  - "regular has arrived" notifications
  - finger signature ([R] card networks dropped the signature requirement in the US around 2018; unverified here)
- **For a grocery till the reusable part of "Square Register"** is the keypad-first custom amount, the item grid laid out like the shelves, and one-tap drawer and print. The custom amount is the touch-screen descendant of the ECR open ring; see the ECR section.

### Gaps
- No source found for the "skeuomorphic register" look or for a dedicated custom-amount keypad screen in the 2010–2012 apps. [R] I recall the original Square app opening on a numeric "charge" keypad, but I did not verify it.
- No Square Market closure date was found.
- Square Register hardware (2017, two screens) and Square Stand's retirement were not researched.
- No Apple Design Award or Red Dot citation was found for any Square product. Only the unsourced MoMA claim appears above.

## Square Wallet / "Pay with Square" (2011–2014), Square Order (2014–2015) and Square Cash for merchants: how pay-by-name worked, why it died, what users missed

### Takeaway
Square Wallet went through three names (Card Case 2011 → Pay with Square, March 2012 → Square Wallet by Dec 2012). The customer opened a "tab" near a merchant, either manually or automatically by geofence, and paid by saying their name. The cashier saw the customer's mandatory photo on the register and confirmed with one tap. The receipt arrived by email or push. Square pulled Wallet from the app stores on 12 May 2014 for Square Order. Order was pulled 13 March 2015 and stopped 20 March 2015.

The causes were too few merchants and consumers, cashiers who had never seen it, unreliable location, privacy ("regulars" showing up on screen as they walked past), and, for Order, no new sales. Square itself called it "a very magical experience but didn't have a lot of the utility value." Regulars at Square coffee shops loved it.

### Cited Findings
- **Card Case, May 2011.** Distributed through merchant links, not publicly listed, iPhone only. A customer within two blocks presses "use tab" and tells the cashier their name. Because Square holds the card, the charge runs on the merchant's register and the customer gets a push notification. Merchants could send offers based on purchase history. [V] — [TechCrunch, 23 May 2011](https://techcrunch.com/2011/05/23/squares-disruptive-new-ipad-payments-service-will-replace-cash-registers/). The cashier would "verify the sale by seeing a picture of the person" [V] — [Engadget, 23 May 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011-05-23-square-kills-the-need-for-nfc-with-virtual-tabs-and-card-case.html). The clerk picks the shopper's name from a list of people logged in nearby, shown with a photo [S] — [Consumer Reports, May 2011](https://www.consumerreports.org/cro/news/2011/05/mobile-wallet-app-lets-you-pay-by-saying-your-name-at-checkout/index.htm).
- **Geofenced auto-tabs, Nov 2011.**

  - "When the customer leaves the defined area, the tab automatically closes" (an email receipt follows)
  - "At the local coffee shop, paying for a latte really was just a matter of telling the barista to put the bill on our tab."

  The automatic version worked on iOS only. [V] — [Entrepreneur, 4 Nov 2011](https://www.entrepreneur.com/blog/220659)
- **Pay with Square, 26 Mar 2012.** It "allows you to create and maintain tabs at local businesses by simply giving the retailer your name". The photo is "mandatory as it is used as a form of ID". Auto-start can "completely minimize the effort exerted at the register". The critic's point: it puts "an awful lot of trust" in staff to recognise faces. - **Square Wallet, Dec 2012** (CES 2013 listing): "Just download Square Wallet, link your credit or debit card and simply say your name to pay." Hands-free checkout; the customer's name and photo appear on the register. [V] — [Shelly Palmer, 10 Dec 2012](https://shellypalmer.com/2012/12/square/)
- **Shutdown, May 2014.** Wallet was pulled from both app stores on 12 May 2014 for Square Order (SF and NYC only). Square said it would support Wallet until Order was everywhere. ### Inferences
- **The magic was recognition plus trust at a place you visit often.** The cashier sees who you are and your history, and paying is a sentence. That is precisely the village-shop or deli "regular". What failed was everything around it: per-merchant enablement, untrained cashiers, GPS, and the privacy of being listed while walking past.
- **For a 2026 Ukrainian grocery,** the reusable moment is a **regulars' tab**: a charge account, the old "write it in the notebook" practice ([R] the notebook practice is common in small Ukrainian shops). The customer is recognised by phone number, loyalty QR or NFC tap rather than geofence, with an optional photo kept only with explicit consent. The balance is settled later by cash or QR. Card-on-file "say your name" charging would need acquirer support for merchant-initiated transactions; that is [R] and unverified.
- **Do not recreate geofenced auto-check-in.** It was unreliable ("half the time it didn't work") and it put passers-by on the merchant's screen.
- **Order-ahead died for lack of incremental sales.** For a grocery it belongs to Telegram or phone pre-orders with a pickup list, not a separate consumer app.

### Gaps
- **Starbucks.** Retail Dive's summary says Starbucks did not adopt Wallet. [R] I recall Starbucks accepting Square Wallet in US stores from about Nov 2012 under the Aug 2012 partnership. This conflict is unresolved here.
- No adoption numbers (users, merchants) for Wallet were found.
- Square Cash for merchants was not researched; the search budget ran out. [R] Square Cash launched about Oct 2013, and business $cashtags arrived about 2015. Unverified.
- The exact date of the rename from Pay with Square to Square Wallet is unverified. It was already "Square Wallet" by 10 Dec 2012.

## NCR Silver (2012–), QuickBooks Desktop POS (discontinued 3 Oct 2023), Microsoft Dynamics RMS (extended support ended 13 Jul 2021): what small retailers and grocers depended on

### Takeaway
- **QuickBooks POS** died because Intuit said its "unique" platform was hard to maintain. Sales stopped 8 Feb 2023 and the product was discontinued 3 Oct 2023. Payments, gift cards, Store Exchange, the vendor lookup and live support all stopped, and Intuit pushed merchants to Shopify. What merchants lost was:
  - the deep QuickBooks Desktop link
  - charge (house) accounts
  - customer price tiers
  - multiple barcodes per item
  - their sales history, which does not migrate
- **Microsoft RMS** was in use at ≥45,000 companies in 2014. It stopped selling in July 2016, mainstream support ended 10 Jul 2016, and extended support ended 13 Jul 2021. Its single-store users found that Microsoft's successor targeted 5+ store chains. The clone that inherited them (RMH) sold itself on "the same features as RMS, including hot keys" and "the same peripheral hardware".
- **NCR Silver** (2012) brought iPad simplicity with integrated scales, loyalty, email marketing and 24/7 live support. Its fate is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **QuickBooks POS ending.** "On October 3, 2023, we discontinued QuickBooks Desktop Point of Sale 19.0 and the following connected services". The list: Payments, Gift Card Service, Mobile Sync, Intuit Store Exchange, Webgility, live support and Vendor Lookup. The reason given: "The platform it runs on is unique, so it's difficult to maintain and add new features." The software keeps running without patches. The Tetra Lane 5000 PIN pad stops working. Gift-card balances become manual. The Shopify migration connector moves products (inventory by location), customers, vendors and notes, but not balances, images or transactions. [V] — [Intuit help article](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/discontinued-offering/quickbooks-desktop-point-sale-discontinuation/L407dYuCG_US_en_US)
- **What QuickBooks POS users depended on, as a POS vendor sees it.** Intuit "stopped selling new licenses on February 8, 2023". "QuickBooks POS earned its loyalty through a deep link to QuickBooks Desktop accounting". Customer loyalty points and history do not transfer. "Transaction and sales history generally does not". ## The classic electronic cash register (Sharp/Casio/NCR/Sam4s) workflow that touch POS lost

### Takeaway
An ECR is a fixed, tactile keypad and nothing else:
- department keys
- open or preset PLUs, keyed by number
- "type the price, hit the department" open rings
- a quantity multiply key
- X (read) and Z (read and reset) reports
- no internet and no subscription

The verified evidence is thin but consistent. A current $279 register still sells with 16 departments, 500 open or preset PLUs and raised keys, and "does not require cloud software". Basic registers cost about $100. Ex-cashiers and POS engineers describe keyboard and numpad ringing as an order of magnitude faster than the GUI POS that replaced it, because you never look at the screen or reach for a mouse. One cash-register engineer adds the counterpoint: stores wanted GUI-plus-softkeys to cut training cost.

### Cited Findings
- **A typical current ECR.** SAM4s ER-180U: "16 programmable departments", "500 open or preset PLUs", a "48-position raised-key keyboard"; it "is not a touchscreen POS system and does not require cloud software"; $279; built for small retail, concession stands, gift shops and farm stands. [V] — [Spartan POS product page](https://spartanpos.com/products/sam4s-er-180u-electronic-cash-register.oembed)
- **Cost argument.** A basic Casio or Sam4S register runs about $100, against about $400 for entry-level POS hardware plus a monthly subscription, which can make POS cost more over ten years [S] — [Expert Market](https://www.expertmarket.com/ca/pos/best-cash-registers-for-small-businesses). Registers suit only "an extremely simple business with few items" [S] — [BuyerZone](https://www.buyerzone.com/retail/industry-insights/cash-register-vs-pos-system/).
- **Speed and muscle memory: ex-cashiers and POS developers (HN, verbatim [U]):**

### Inferences
- **What touch POS lost.** All four points below are inferred from the quotes above. [R] The ECR flow described here is general knowledge, not checked against a manual in this session.
  - **Price-first entry.** Digits, then the department key, then the next item. No modal "custom item" dialog, no category drill-down, no keyboard popping up.
  - **Fixed physical positions,** so a cashier rings by feel while handling goods.
  - **Numeric codes as a first-class lookup:** PLU number, then the PLU key.
  - **The end-of-day ritual of X then Z.**
- **The engineer's point matters too.** The hybrid (fixed keys or softkeys whose labels change per screen) beat both pure keypads and pure GUIs for large stores, because it kept speed while lowering training cost.
- **Grocery-specific.** Open rings by department and PLU-by-number are how loose bread, produce, deli and weighed goods are sold when they carry no barcode. The abuse vector ("rang their own lunch... as bananas") argues for a permission on open rings, a cap, and an "open-ring share per cashier" line in the Z/X report.

**Why each died, in one line (inferred from the findings above):**
- Square Wallet and Order: network-effect apps without enough merchants, and no incremental sales.
- ShopKeep, Vend, Kounta, Breadcrumb, Upserve: absorbed into Lightspeed's flagships or divested.
- QuickBooks POS and RMS: desktop platforms their owners stopped maintaining.
- Leaf: hardware absorbed by Heartland, then defunct.
- Poynt and iZettle: absorbed by GoDaddy and PayPal; they live on under new names.

**What users missed afterwards (verified):**
- Square Wallet: the "regular" experience ("Wallet is totally awesome" at your own coffee shop) and hands-free payment.
- QuickBooks POS: the accounting link, charge accounts, price tiers, multi-barcodes and history.
- RMS: hot keys and support for the same hardware, which is what its clone marketed.

### Gaps
- **Fiscal fit of a department open ring.** Whether a generic department name is an acceptable goods name on a Ukrainian fiscal receipt must be checked against Положення № 13 and the team's ПРРО docs before row 1 ships. The rules for the electronic fiscal receipt (row 8) need the same check.
- **Payments.** Whether Ukrainian acquirers support card-on-file merchant-initiated charges for a "say your name" tab is not researched.
- **Scales and barcodes.** Requirements for legally verified trade scales, and the format of weight-embedded barcodes, are [R].
- **Grocer voice.** No first-hand interviews or forum threads from small grocers (US or Ukrainian) were available in this session. A follow-up should collect them, for example through Ukrainian shop-owner Facebook or Telegram groups, or Reddit through a reachable proxy that the rules allow.
